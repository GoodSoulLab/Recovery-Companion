# Recovery Companion — Product Lab

Research and prototype workspace for evaluating whether a privacy-conscious, clinically governed, user-directed recovery companion should exist.

**Status:** Product discovery. No clinical validation, participant findings, or commercial validation. Draft exercises are for qualified review only.

## Current decision

Test a narrow hypothesis before making a venture or product commitment: does a compact, evidence-labeled resource bank help adults voluntarily seeking change find an acceptable next step and understand clinical versus peer support better than a plain resource list or the routes they already use?

The current public-source evidence shows that integration alone is not a defensible novelty claim. Outbound links improve basic usefulness but are also replicable; any advantage would need to come from reviewed, maintained, clearly bounded curation. Relay publicly describes plans, therapist-designed lessons, check-ins, urge/trigger logging, and peer support; SMART Recovery offers free tools and meetings. These are publisher claims, not authenticated app tests, independent audits, or clinical outcome evidence.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Adults who voluntarily seek behavior change; no diagnosis required.
- Do not pathologize high libido, pornography use, moral incongruence, or a screening score.
- No passive sexual/browsing surveillance, unrestricted AI therapy, automatic disclosure, compulsory abstinence, or trauma processing.
- Keep peer support voluntary and independent; do not present the prototype as treatment, monitoring, or crisis response.
- Exercise drafts remain unreviewed until qualified clinical and lived-experience review is recorded.
- No participant research until safety, privacy, intellectual-property, accessibility, and ethics gates are met.
- Preserve null results; stop or pivot if existing alternatives are sufficient.

## Current gate

Prototype v0.9 is a moment-led, ten-resource pocket guide. Four voluntary entry choices route to an evidence-labeled shelf; people can also browse all resources directly. Optional browser-local saving stores saved resource IDs only. The UX decision v0.3 challenges the routing hypothesis, compares it with a flat shelf, and defines an eight-person formative falsification study. The new research note separates outbound pathways for peer meetings, sponsorship, and professional care, with source limits and no-referrer link behavior. This is a working design direction, not validated user research, clinical approval, or evidence of benefit. Source checks cover routes, filters, storage scope, reduced motion, direct HTTPS links, and no network requests. Browser/assistive-technology, clinical, lived-experience, privacy/security, ethics, accessibility, and IP reviews remain open. Do not expose exercise content to participants until the documented gates are resolved.
