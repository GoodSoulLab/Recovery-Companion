# Recovery Companion — Project Handoff
Date: 2026-10-11
Status: Product discovery; no validated clinical or commercial claims.

## Purpose of the new project
Explore, prototype, and critically validate a privacy-first, clinically governed, user-directed companion for adults voluntarily seeking help with unwanted sexual behaviors. It may combine original/reviewed ACT-, DBT-, CBT-informed skills, opt-in 12-step/peer practices, self-chosen boundaries, and voluntary human connection. The opportunity is **appropriate intervention selection and practical action in the moment**, not mere feature aggregation.

## Research history and pivots
1. Broad CSBD/problematic pornography use landscape reviewed: WHO ICD-11 distinguishes clinically significant impaired control from high libido and moral disapproval alone. Screening-positive does not mean diagnosed or seeking treatment.
2. Considered treatment access, clinician navigation, peer recovery, continuing care, and provider infrastructure. Evidence does not support assuming all non-treatment is unmet demand.
3. Reviewed SAA/12-step, SMART Recovery, psychotherapy, trauma-informed approaches, and alternatives. Free peer support and reduced-fee psychotherapy already exist. Standalone navigation thesis placed on hold pending direct access validation.
4. New thesis: integrate evidence-informed skills, personal recovery practices, and optional human connection into a context-sensitive single-pane-of-glass experience.
5. Competitive challenge: Relay, Fortify, Brainbuddy and others already combine several of these capabilities. Do not claim to be first integrated recovery app. Public descriptions are not authenticated testing or proof of clinical outcomes.
6. Current experiment: compare **static toolkit** versus **context-sensitive selection interface**, with the same reviewed exercises. Evaluate selection fit, time, perceived usefulness, agency, shame/distress, privacy confidence, and safety. No clinical efficacy claim from usability testing.

## Current product boundaries
- Adults 18+ voluntarily seeking behavior change; no diagnosis required.
- Do not equate pornography use, high libido, moral incongruence, infidelity, or screening scores with CSBD.
- No automated diagnosis, PTSD/trauma processing, unrestricted AI therapist, passive sexual/browsing surveillance, automatic sponsor/partner disclosures, or compulsory abstinence/12-step framing.
- Start with user-initiated moments: urges, emotional overwhelm, setbacks. Expand later to isolation, relationships, and values-based life activities.
- Tool suggestions must be explainable, rejectable, clinically reviewed, and sensitive to user preferences. Trauma-informed principles apply across the product, not as an unvalidated universal causal theory.
- Original exercises or appropriately licensed content; Facing the Shadow worksheets and other branded materials require IP review.
- No sensitive account or sexual-history intake for first prototype. Prefer ephemeral/local state, neutral notifications, granular opt-in sharing, deletion and export controls, independent privacy/security review.

## Questions that could kill the thesis
- Do existing products already solve the moment-of-need problem adequately?
- Does context-sensitive selection outperform a good static toolkit, or merely feel sophisticated?
- Is there a real, consent-respecting privacy advantage rather than marketing language?
- Can cross-tradition integration remain clinically coherent and noncoercive?
- Are safety, review, content licensing, and distribution costs sustainable?
- Can value be demonstrated in real-world functioning without rewarding app dependence or shame-based engagement?

## Operating discipline
Separate facts, hypotheses, design proposals, and unverified vendor claims. Challenge assumptions and compare against strong existing alternatives. Do not commission broad research without a decision-critical question. No new product features without an identified user job, safety rationale, and testable hypothesis. 'No venture' remains a valid conclusion.
