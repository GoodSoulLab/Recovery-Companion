# Recovery Companion — Staged Review Commission Plan v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** Planning scope only. No reviewer has been contacted, no budget approved, and no work commissioned.  
**Applies to:** [Prototype v0.4](../../prototype/context-selector-v0.4.html) and [Reviewer and Formative Test Protocol v0.2](REVIEW_PROTOCOL_v0.2.md)

## Recommendation

Use a staged review budget. First commission a bounded review of the exact intervention content, safety language, and experience from:

1. one currently practicing, appropriately licensed clinician with directly relevant experience; and
2. two paid lived-experience partners with different self-chosen behavior-change goals.

This phase is for detecting content, framing, and agency problems. Two partners are not a representative sample and cannot establish acceptability across the intended population. Do not recruit research participants or describe the prototype as safe, effective, or clinically approved based on this review.

If the content remains viable after this phase, scope privacy/security, accessibility, intellectual-property, and ethics work separately. All applicable gates must be resolved before participant exposure.

## Phase 1 scope of work

### Clinical reviewer

**Qualifications to verify before selection**

- Current, unrestricted license and good standing in the relevant jurisdiction.
- Active clinical practice and experience with adults who voluntarily seek behavior change.
- Relevant experience with compulsive sexual behavior or adjacent concerns, including ability to distinguish impaired control from distress grounded only in moral conflict.
- Willingness to identify limits, conflicts, and any areas requiring specialist review.

**Materials**

- The exact v0.4 card text and metadata.
- Safety, stop, support, and non-monitoring language.
- The non-graphic scenario tasks and draft moderator script in Protocol v0.2.
- A concise statement of the product boundaries.

**Requested deliverables**

- Per-card decision: approve for this narrow usability task, revise, reject, or escalate.
- Specific rationale and minimum required revisions for any blocked card or phrase.
- Review of scenario-task and moderator wording for stigma, coercion, false reassurance, unsafe concealment, excessive authority, or missed referral boundaries.
- One bounded re-review of revised language to confirm that required changes were made.
- A brief written statement of scope and limits. Do not ask the reviewer to certify efficacy or guarantee safety.

**Quote request format**

Request a fixed fee for document review, one 45–60 minute discussion, and one bounded re-review. Ask the clinician to state assumptions, excluded work, turnaround, conflicts, and any additional expertise needed. If fixed-fee work is unavailable, request an hourly rate and a not-to-exceed estimate before work begins.

**Planning estimate, not market rate:** allow approximately 3–5 hours total for the initial read, discussion, and one short re-review. Revise this estimate after a reviewer sees the actual card packet.

### Lived-experience partners

Invite two adults who independently self-identify relevant lived experience and voluntarily sought behavior change. Seek different goals and perspectives; do not require diagnosis, abstinence, a recovery label, testimonials, or disclosure of personal histories. Do not identify or approach people through private support-group membership.

Offer separate confidential sessions or a private written-feedback route. Avoid a group session for the first review so that partners are not asked to hear one another’s personal context. Each person may review the same exact materials and comment on:

- wording that feels blaming, pressuring, confusing, or falsely reassuring;
- whether options feel rejectable and whether “none” remains a valid choice;
- whether the experience respects varied goals and identities;
- whether any prompt requests more context than it needs;
- burden, comprehension, and whether human support is easy to find;
- any omitted concern or unintended meaning.

**Requested participation**

- A plain-language overview and the exact materials in advance.
- Approximately 30–45 minutes of private preparation, a 60–75 minute session or equivalent written response, and up to 30 minutes for follow-up clarification if they choose.
- A clear agreement describing tasks, compensation amount and timing, expense reimbursement, how feedback will be used, privacy limits, and withdrawal options.
- The option to comment without giving a personal example; no audio/video recording by default.

**Compensation**

Pay for preparation, review, and agreed follow-up, whether or not feedback is favorable. Agree on amount, method, timing, and access-cost reimbursement before the work starts, and discuss any payment format or benefit implications privately with the partner. Do not make payment depend on completion of every question or on endorsement.

PCORI’s compensation guidance advises teams to consider role, responsibility, time, expertise, comparable roles, and living wage; PCORI explicitly does not set a universal rate. It also recommends discussing partner preferences early and allocating funds for access expenses. This is process guidance, not a required rate card for this project:

- https://www.pcori.org/engagement-research/tools-and-resources-support-engagement-research/pcori-compensation-framework
- https://www.pcori.org/engagement-research/engagement-resources/foundational-expectations/dedicated-funds-engagement-partner-compensation

**Planning estimate, not a compensation rate:** budget each partner for about 2–2.5 hours, plus reasonable agreed access expenses. Ask partners whether the scope and compensation are acceptable; adjust the scope, amount, or format before inviting them to begin. If two reviewers reveal conflicting or sharply divergent experiences, consider adding one or two partners before deciding the content is ready.

## Phase 1 exit decision

Proceed to design revision only if there is no unresolved material safety, coercion, stigma, or privacy-comprehension concern. For each card, retain a versioned disposition log. Exclude cards that cannot be revised within scope. If reviewers’ feedback suggests users primarily need human support, a static directory, or no tool, pause the selector thesis rather than adding features.

A “continue” decision authorizes only the next internal preparation stage. It does **not** authorize participant recruitment or testing.

## Phase 2: only after Phase 1 passes

Obtain scoped proposals for:

- **Privacy/security:** exact artifact and delivery-path review, runtime request/storage checks, host/device exposure, and study-data workflow.
- **Accessibility:** rendered-browser keyboard, zoom/reflow, and screen-reader evaluation of the exact build.
- **IP/provenance:** card-level rights check; specialist advice only for unresolved questions.
- **Ethics:** determine whether IRB or other ethics oversight applies and document the resulting requirements.

Set the Phase 2 ceiling only after the relevant artifact and delivery plan are known. Do not spend on participant recruitment, analytics, hosting integrations, or clinical-effect claims at this stage.

## Draft clinician quote request

**Subject:** Paid, time-limited clinical review of a recovery-support prototype

I’m preparing a small, expert-only prototype for adults who voluntarily seek behavior change. I’m seeking a practicing clinician with relevant experience to review 12 original draft exercises, safety language, and fictional task prompts. This is a request for a bounded design review, not treatment delivery, efficacy validation, or a safety certification.

The proposed scope is a line-by-line review, one 45–60 minute discussion, and one bounded re-review after required edits. Please let me know your relevant experience, current license/jurisdiction, any conflicts, availability, fixed-fee or hourly terms, assumptions, and what you would exclude or require before accepting this scope. No participant data or personal histories are involved.

## Draft lived-experience invitation

**Subject:** Paid private review of a draft behavior-change tool

I’m looking for two adults who have voluntarily sought to change an unwanted behavior to review a draft tool’s language and usability. You would review fictional, non-graphic scenarios and draft exercises, then share what feels clear, respectful, useful, pressuring, or missing. You do not need to share your own story, diagnosis, sexual history, or recovery label. Different goals are welcome.

The review is private, approximately two hours including preparation, and compensated whether or not your feedback is positive. You may skip any item or stop. Before you decide, I’ll share the materials, exact tasks, compensation amount and timing, privacy limits, and how feedback will be used. No audio or video recording is planned. Would you like the details?
