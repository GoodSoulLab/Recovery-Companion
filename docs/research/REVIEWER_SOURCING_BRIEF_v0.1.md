# Reviewer sourcing brief v0.1

**Prepared:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** Desk research only. No one has been contacted or endorsed. The current study artifact is prototype v0.4; see [Reviewer and Formative Test Protocol v0.2](REVIEW_PROTOCOL_v0.2.md) for the aligned test gates and decision rule.  
**Purpose:** Identify the smallest qualified review group for the expert-only prototype and clarify what public credentials do and do not establish.

## Decision

Do not treat an academic subject-matter review as clinical safety approval. Before any participant-facing test, the minimum review group should include:

1. **A currently practicing, appropriately licensed clinician** with experience supporting adults who voluntarily seek help changing a behavior, familiarity with compulsive behavior / loss-of-control presentations, and an explicitly non-stigmatizing approach that distinguishes impairment from distress driven only by moral conflict.
2. **Two to four paid lived-experience partners** with varied goals and perspectives, recruited by opt-in invitation. They review usability, language, burden, agency, and perceived safety; they are not asked to disclose histories or certify clinical safety.
3. **A product/research owner** who records disagreement and resolves copy changes before testing. A clinician’s review does not replace ethics review where research regulations apply.

The public profiles below are a shortlist for possible *subject-matter consultation*, not a recommendation to appoint any person as the treating clinician, safety officer, or sole authority. Clinical and lived-experience review are only part of the pre-exposure gates: the exact build also needs privacy/security and accessibility review, an IP/provenance check, and a documented ethics determination. These are separate functions; one reviewer’s approval does not satisfy them all. Their availability, fees, current clinical practice, conflicts, and willingness are unknown. Verify each directly before an invitation.

### Clinician selection requirements

If none of the subject-matter candidates is available or suitable, source an independent practicing clinician through a professional association, academic clinic, or qualified clinical network. Before appointment, verify:

- current license and good standing in the clinician’s jurisdiction;
- scope of practice and experience with adults voluntarily seeking behavior change;
- experience with the specific behavior domain and adjacent concerns (e.g., mood, trauma, compulsivity, sexual health where applicable);
- ability to review for stigma, moral incongruence, coercion, crisis escalation, and referral boundaries;
- conflicts of interest, commercial ties, framework commitments, and any financial relationship to competing interventions;
- paid scope, deliverables, turnaround, and whether the reviewer is giving design feedback or formal clinical governance.

One reviewer should not be asked to validate efficacy. Clinical copy review can identify foreseeable risks; it cannot establish that an intervention works.

## Lived-experience review route

Do not publish or infer named individuals’ recovery status. Use a short, transparent, opt-in invitation through appropriate independent communities or an established advisory / peer network after checking its participation rules. No direct outreach to support-group members and no recruiting from a group without its organization’s permission.

For an initial review, invite **two to four adults** who self-identify as having voluntarily sought to change a relevant behavior. Offer choice across goals (reduction, abstinence, moderation, or another self-defined goal), time since seeking change, and relevant identities or contexts. Do not require a diagnosis, a recovery label, abstinence, a public testimonial, or detailed personal disclosures. Interested people may self-describe only what is relevant to the review; allow anonymous input and withdrawal without explanation.

Pay for preparation and review time, agree the amount and method up front, reimburse access costs where needed, and make participation independent of positive feedback. Give partners a clear role, a plain-language description of how their feedback will be used, and an option to review summaries attributed to them. Do not use their words, stories, or identities in public materials without separate, explicit permission.

PCORI’s engagement standards call for representative involvement, early and ongoing engagement, dedicated funds and fair compensation, meaningful decision inclusion, and ongoing assessment. A recent scoping review of lived-experience community advisory boards also identifies clear roles, appropriate compensation, mutual respect, and attention to power dynamics as recurring practices. These sources support the engagement process; they do not prove the right sample size or guarantee representativeness.

- [PCORI Foundational Expectations for Partnerships in Research](https://www.pcori.org/resources/pcoris-foundational-expectations-partnerships-research)

## Proposed review session

**Format:** 60–75 minute remote session, compensated; option for private written feedback instead.  
**Materials:** the expert-only prototype, this project’s safety boundaries, and synthetic non-graphic scenarios only.  
**Do not collect:** diagnosis, detailed sexual/behavioral histories, relapse details, trauma narratives, identifiable health information, or free-form story submissions.  
**Questions:** Can a person understand and control the suggested exercise? Does any copy shame, diagnose, pressure disclosure, imply guaranteed results, obscure options, or make human support harder to reach? What feels missing for people whose goals differ?  
**Record:** issue-level observations, consented quotations only, reviewer role (not identity unless requested), design response, and unresolved disagreement. Keep reviewer contact details and compensation records outside the public repository.

## Research boundaries and gates

- The current prototype is concept material for expert review only. It is not a clinical intervention, validated assessment, or participant-facing study instrument.
- No clinical claim, safety claim, treatment recommendation, or efficacy claim follows from this desk research.
- The candidate list is based on public professional profiles, not personal contact, private data, or a conflict investigation.
- Reviewer feedback can guide a revision. It cannot substitute for the separate privacy/security and accessibility reviews, IP/provenance check, ethics determination, or a properly designed falsification experiment.
- Keep participant recruitment and sensitive research on hold until the review protocol and governance are accepted by qualified reviewers.
- This repository is public and MIT-licensed. This brief intentionally contains only public professional facts and process guidance. Do not place reviewer contact details, private correspondence, compensation terms, or participant data here.
